# Lab Tasks
>[!EXAMPLE] Task 1 - Computational Literature Examination
>Examine computational intelligence literature to uncover whether the following tasks can be solved by computers:
>- Driving around London City Centre
>- Translating spoken English
>- Writing an intentionally funny story
>- Buying a week's worth of groceries on the Web
>- Playing a decent game of table tennis (ping pong)
>- Discovering and proving new mathematical theorems
>- Performing complex surgical operations
>- Giving competent legal advice in a specialised area of the law
>
>For the infeasible tasks - Explore what difficulties are and predict when, and if ever they will be overcome.

# Lab Work
>[!EXAMPLE] Task 1 - Computation Literature Examination
>>[!DONE] Driving around London City Centre
>> A paper (Hawke et al. 2021) questions whether all the fundamental problems of autonomous vehicles have been solved, acknowledging that at present during their research, 4 years ago in 2021, deemed the problem not feasible due to an a few prime factors as directly quoted:
> >
> > >[!QUOTE]
> > >1) technical scalability
> > >2) safety-critical engineering effort
> > >3) unit economics (profitability of an AV based on utilisation and costs)
> > >4) Regulation
> >
> >"technical scalability" is defined in the paper as:
> >
> > >[!QUOTE] 
> > >the ability of the decision-making software systems to [generalise] to new situations quickly with sufficient performance for deployment." 
> >
> >This means that the software on-board autonomous vehicle needs to have critical-level decision-making abilities. For instance, avoiding or understanding how to properly cause the most none or the most minimal harm during a trolley problem like situation in unseen environments (i.e. London), and prevent accident or harm to the driver or passenger(s) while maintaining its ability and speed to recognise these situations is adequately quick enough that it is suitable for mainstream mass deployment to the public, and properly alert the user when it is unable to, so they can take control. 
> 
> >[!DONE] Translating Spoken English
> >This task is solved, as written in a paper from Seamless Communication (2023), which writes about the existing solution for 
> > >[!QUOTE]
> > >"X2T Into-Text Translation [&] Transcription" demonstrating how machine learning models with "trained on X-eng directions".
> >
> >- X2T = X-To-Text (X can either be Text or Speech)
> >- X-Eng = X-To-English (X is any source of language)
> >
> >The idea demonstrating how pre-trained models when used with the SeamlessM4T model can translate audio from speech-to-text which utilise
> >
> > >[!QUOTE]
> > >"[A] [conformer-based encoder](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2020/gulati20_interspeech.html) for speech input" and a Transformer-based Encoder ([[NIPS-2017-attention-is-all-you-need-Paper.pdf]]) for text input"
>
> > [!WARNING] Writing an Intentionally Funny Story
> >This task continues to pose many challenges to humanity, the creation of humorous content as demonstrated in a paper by (Loakman et al. 2025) details the challenges and existing ways that have been attempted to create humour or at least the perception of it, through natural language processing. 
> >
> >The paper claims that the generation of humour computationally is highly "context-dependent" and requires an "extensive [level of] reasoning" to understand and create.
